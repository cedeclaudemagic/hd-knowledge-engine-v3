Priya stood up. Walked him through the app. Answered questions. Completed the transaction. He left, still confused but participating.

She sat back down. "That's the 25th new person."

"I was counting," Simon said.

"Is that bad journalism? Participating in the story you're documenting?"

"Probably. But I don't know how to observe this without being in it. Every time I'm here, I'm changing what's happening. Even just asking questions changes how you think about what you're doing."

"So what do we do with that?"

"Document it honestly. Including the fact that I don't know if I'm observing or participating. Including the fact that you don't know if you made a good decision or a desperate one."

Priya looked at her spreadsheet again. "Can I tell you what's really scary?"

"Yeah."

"It's not whether this is economically viable. We've been crunching those numbers for three years. Hybrid was working. Local-only might work if circulation increases. The scary part is: I don't know if I did this for good reasons or bad ones. Did I push to full local currency because it was the right next step? Or because I needed to feel like I was doing something radical? Because I was tired of being in the gap and wanted to resolve it one way or another?"

Simon was very quiet. Then: "I think that's the most honest thing anyone's said at a Tuesday gathering."

"Yeah?"

"Yeah. Because that's what everyone's actually asking themselves. Ed didn't leave The Sentinel because he had a plan. He left because he couldn't function in the gap anymore. Marcus didn't quit JPMorgan with an alternative ready. He quit because the performance was killing him. Richard didn't choose to get fired. But now he's here anyway. And none of you know if you made good decisions or just human ones."

"Is there a difference?"

"I don't know. That's what I'm trying to understand by documenting all of this."

Priya's phone buzzed. Customer question. She answered it. Then another. Then someone at the door needing help with the app.

Simon watched her work. Saturday morning foot traffic. People coming in. Some with the app already. Some downloading it. Some leaving confused. The messy reality of an experiment in progress.

When there was a lull, he asked: "So what happens if this doesn't work? If the exchange fund drains? If you can't pay suppliers? If you have to close?"

"Then I fail."

"And?"

"And I'll have documented why. You'll have documented why. Other people can learn from it. Try something different. Maybe the answer is hybrid is the sweet spot. Maybe the answer is we need more local suppliers first. Maybe the answer is the whole thing is impossible. But at least we'll know more than we know now."

"That's not very comforting."

"No. But it's honest." She paused. "Can I ask you something?"

"Yeah."

"In your documentation - the thing you're writing - are you trying to prove something? That this works or doesn't work? That we're right or wrong?"

"No. I'm trying to show what it looks like when people try to build something different without knowing if it's possible. Because I think that's the information people need. Not 'here's the answer' but 'here's what exploring the question actually looks like.'"

"Even if we're making mistakes?"

"Especially if you're making mistakes. That's the most useful information."

Priya nodded slowly. "Okay. Then publish all of it. The numbers that don't quite work. The questions I can't answer. The fact that I might have jumped for psychological reasons more than economic ones. All of it."

"You're sure?"

"No. But I'm doing it anyway."

Another customer came in. Then another. Saturday continuing. The co-op functioning in its uncertain way.

Simon left at 11 AM. Priya stayed, serving customers, answering questions, watching the numbers accumulate.

73% exchange rate. 27% local circulation. Was that better than 40% local circulation with hybrid? She genuinely didn't know.

But 25 new participants. 25 more people experiencing redistribution. 25 more nodes in a network that might be growing or might be an elaborate way to fail more publicly.

She'd find out.

They'd all find out.

Perhaps.
