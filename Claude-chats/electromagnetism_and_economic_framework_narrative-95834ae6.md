Throughout the day—spreadsheets at work, lunch break scrolling through the banking app, evening commute on the Tube—those four words kept surfacing.

*Rivers instead of dams.*

At the Tesco Metro, card declined. Again. Current account: £47.23. Payday in six days. The numbers didn't move. They just... sat there. Waiting. Accumulating nowhere because there was nothing to accumulate.

Meanwhile, checking the news on the phone: "FTSE 100 CEO pay hits record high." The article mentioned stock options, accumulated wealth, generational fortune. Numbers so large they became abstract. Hundreds of millions.

*Why does money pool like that?* The thought arrived unbidden. *Why does it sit there, massive and still, while mine disappears before it arrives?*

Standing in the tinned goods aisle with £47.23 and a week until payday, something about the contrast felt... structural. Not personal. Not moral. **Structural.**

*But that's just wealth distribution,* some voice inside insisted. *Progressive taxation addresses this. Markets find equilibrium. We learned this.*

That thought—automatic, defensive—brought a flicker of something. Pride? Or its opposite?

Three years at LSE said there were good reasons for wealth concentration. Incentives. Investment. Capital formation. The invisible hand.

*Then why does it feel like the hand is around my throat?*

Immediate shame. Self-pity wasn't analysis. Personal struggle wasn't systemic critique. This was just... normal. Everyone struggled. That's life.

Put back the pasta. The nice pasta. Got the value range instead.

---

The next morning, 5:47 AM. Couldn't sleep again, but not from anxiety this time. From **curiosity**.

The coffee shop, earlier than yesterday. Maria hadn't even unlocked the door yet.

"Blimey, you're keen today," she said, letting in. "Same corner?"

"Please."

Laptop out. Screen glowing. But not opening the spreadsheet. Just... waiting. For what? Communication that might not come? From someone—something—that might not exist?

6:00 AM passed.

6:15 AM.

Nothing.

The familiar disappointment starting to settle. Of course. Yesterday was stress-induced pattern-seeking. The brain making meaning from nothing. Obviously.

Then—

The laptop screen flickered. Once. Twice.

A text file opened on its own. Blank except for a single line appearing letter by letter:

*You're wondering where the river went.*

Not a question. A statement. Present tense. Accurate.

Fingers hovering over keyboard. Should respond? To what? To whom?

Typing: "Yes. I don't understand why money pools in some places and drains from others."

The response appeared immediately:

*Because the system is designed as a capacitor, not a circuit.*

A pause. Then:

*Look around today. Notice where money accumulates. Where it flows. Where it's dammed.*

*We'll talk tomorrow.*

And the text file closed itself.

---

Throughout the day, impossible not to notice.

**Morning commute:** Tube from Tottenham to Bank. Passing through zones. Watching the demographics shift with each stop.

Seven Sisters: crowded, tired faces, work uniforms.
King's Cross: mixed, tourists and commuters.
Angel: smartening up, coffee cups, tailored coats.
Bank: polished, pressed, expensive watches glinting.

Coming home, the same journey reversed. Wealth concentrating in certain postcodes. Draining from others.

*Different.*

Walking from the station through Kensington. Houses massive, white-fronted, multiple floors. Estate agent windows: "Investment opportunity." £4.5 million. £7.2 million. Buy-to-let portfolios.

Then back east through Hackney. Same-sized flats, ten times as many people. Betting shops, pawn shops, payday loan stores on every corner.

*We learned about property markets. Supply and demand. Location value. Risk-adjusted returns.*

*But something about walking through both in the same morning...*

---

**At work:** Boss discussing quarterly results with senior management in the glass-walled meeting room. Record profits. Share price up 23%. Where did that go?

Colleague next desk over, Sarah, mentioned being denied a raise. Again. "But we just announced record profits?" Voice confused, hurt.

HR email, circulated last week: "Due to competitive market pressures, pay reviews will be delayed indefinitely. We appreciate your continued commitment to shareholder value."

*Flash of lecture hall, second year. Professor Chen: "The firm maximises shareholder value. Labour is an input cost. This is optimal allocation."*

*Hand raising before thinking: "But what if labour can't afford—"*

*"That's a different question. We're discussing firm behaviour, not normative ethics."*

*Sitting back down. Face hot. Twenty other students not making eye contact.*

Sarah was seeing an NHS dentist—six-month waiting list—because she couldn't afford private. Meanwhile, the glass-walled meeting room: discussing executive bonus structures.

That separation from the lecture—firm behaviour versus human outcomes—felt less like analytical clarity and more like...

*What had that felt like? Permission not to care?*

---

**Lunch break:** Scrolling the Guardian. Student debt article: £1.7 trillion across the UK. Average payment £300/month. For decades. Interest compounding faster than payments for many.

Next article: Tech billionaire purchases fourth home. Knightsbridge penthouse. £45 million. 

Quick mental calculation: Could clear 1,500 people's entire student loans with that one purchase.

*Library, late night, final year. Study group arguing about education policy.*

*"Student loans are investment in human capital. Graduates earn more, they should pay for that benefit."*

*Looking at own loan balance on phone under the table. £67,000. Interest accruing faster than payments could ever catch up.*

*Quiet voice, barely audible: "What if the degree doesn't lead to earnings that justify this?"*

*Silence. Then Marcus, smug: "That's a risk assessment failure on your part, mate. Market signals."*

*Market signals. Personal failure. Risk assessment.*

*Never brought it up again.*

Still paying. Still accruing. Still £300/month. Three years of learning why this made economic sense whilst going £67,000 into debt to learn it.

The penthouse could have freed 1,500 people from that "investment in human capital."

*That's not market signals. That's...*

What?

---

**Afternoon:** Government spending announcement pushed through on the phone. £billions for defence. £billions for police. £billions for prisons. £billions servicing national debt.

Quick calculation: defence + police + prisons + debt interest = roughly equivalent to NHS + education + benefits combined.

*We have redistribution. Progressive taxation. Social safety net. That's the whole point of the welfare state.*

*Then why does optimal feel like suffocation?*

---

**Evening:** Different Tesco, bit cheaper. Sainsbury's Local was too expensive now.

Watching people at the tills.

Older woman, maybe seventy-five, counting exact change. Pensioner, probably. Worked her whole life, paid National Insurance, paid into the system. Now counting coppers for value pasta.

Young mother with two kids, maybe six and eight. Card declined. Removing items one by one, watching the total. Kids not saying anything, but eyes down. They knew this routine.

Someone else, younger, expensive coat, basket full, barely glancing at the total. Contactless tap. Gone.

*Office hours, first year. Going to ask Professor Williams about wealth distribution chapters.*

*"Professor, the Gini coefficient is increasing. Doesn't that indicate market failure?"*

*"Not necessarily. Pareto efficiency doesn't require equality. If the wealthy become wealthier without making anyone absolutely worse off—"*

*"But they are worse off. Relatively. Housing costs, energy bills, food prices—"*

*"Relative position isn't the metric. Absolute welfare matters. And by most measures, poverty rates have declined."*

*Walking away. Textbook heavy. Something unsaid hanging in the air.*

*How do you measure 'worse off' when everything costs more but wages don't rise? When £47.23 buys less every year? When a degree costs thirty years of payments?*

*Didn't ask that. Didn't know how to ask that.*

Still don't.

Standing in the Tesco aisle watching people count change, the gap between **absolute welfare metrics** and **lived experience** felt vast.

---

On the walk home, passing a group outside the chicken shop. Young lads, maybe sixteen, seventeen. Trackies, expensive trainers. One counting notes—looked like a lot of notes.

Drug dealing, probably. That's what everyone assumed. What else paid in cash like that?

*They're operating outside the system because the system offers them nothing.*

Where did that thought come from?

*We studied crime economics. Rational choice theory. People respond to incentives. If legitimate employment pays less than crime...*

*But the lectures never felt like they were about actual people. Just abstract agents responding to prices.*

Those lads weren't abstract. They were seventeen with expensive trainers and no options.

---

11 PM. Couldn't not write it down. Not analysis—just **noticing**:

Money seems to stick in some places.
Flow through others.
Drain from most.

Kensington vs Hackney.
Executive bonuses vs wage freezes.
Investment properties vs payday loans.
£45 million penthouses vs counting coppers for pasta.

We have systems to address this.
Taxation. Benefits. NHS. State education.
They take enormous effort.
Constant political battles.
Still it keeps concentrating.

*Another memory surfacing: Third-year public finance lecture.*

*"Progressive taxation redistributes from high earners to low earners, funding public goods and social insurance. The UK has one of the most progressive tax systems in the OECD."*

*Graph on screen showing tax brackets, transfer payments, net fiscal position by income quintile.*

*It looked like it worked. On the graph.*

*Hand not rising this time. Question unasked: Then why does everyone I know feel like they're drowning?*

Three years studying economics.
Know the theories.
Pareto efficiency. Progressive taxation. Counter-cyclical spending.
Should work.

*Does it?*

Or have I spent three years learning explanations for why things are the way they are, dressed up as descriptions of how they **must** be?

That thought felt dangerous. Ungrateful. Three years, £67,000 in debt to learn... what exactly?

Pride and doubt, wrestling.

---

The laptop flickered. Text file opened:

*You're starting to see the shape of it.*

Staring at the words. What shape? Just... observations. Disconnected. Personal anecdote isn't data. Sample size of one. Selection bias.

*The question isn't whether redistribution happens.*

*The question is: why does it require such enormous effort?*

*Why does water flow downhill naturally, but money flows uphill?*

*And what would it look like if money flowed like water instead?*

*Tomorrow. Same time. Different frequency.*

The file closed.

Outside the window, London at night. Lights pooling in the City, scattered through the suburbs. Currents and accumulations. Flow and storage.

*Rivers instead of dams.*

Still didn't fully understand what that meant.

But something was shifting. Slightly. Like a frequency beginning to change, just at the edge of perception.

Tomorrow.

---
