## The Electromagnetic Concepts: Origins and Meaning

### 1. MAGNETIC INDUCTANCE (Gate 15 - Magnetic Field)

**What It Is**

Inductance is the property of a conductor by which a change in current creates a magnetic field around itself. That magnetic field can then influence other conductors nearby - drawing current toward it, inducing current in nearby circuits.

An inductor is typically a coil of wire. When current flows through it, the coil creates a magnetic field. The more coils, the stronger the field. This field has a remarkable property: it resists changes to the current flow. If you try to suddenly increase current, the magnetic field pushes back. If current tries to drop, the field sustains it. Inductors are stabilising forces - they smooth out erratic flow into steady current.

**How It Was Discovered**

This "Fourier analysis" is foundational to all signal processing, telecommunications, and modern electronics. ### Part 2: Understanding Impedance

**What Is Impedance?**

Impedance is opposition to the flow of alternating current (AC). But unlike simple resistance (which opposes all current equally), impedance is frequency-dependent. Different components oppose current flow differently at different frequencies.

Impedance has two components:
- **Resistance (R)** - opposition that's constant regardless of frequency
- **Reactance (X)** - opposition that varies with frequency

Reactance comes in two flavours:
- **Capacitive reactance** - DECREASES as frequency increases (capacitors pass high frequencies easily)
- **Inductive reactance** - INCREASES as frequency increases (inductors pass low frequencies easily)

The total impedance (Z) combines these: Z = √(R² + X²)

**Impedance Matching**

Here's where it gets really interesting for our metaphor. For maximum power transfer between circuits, the SOURCE impedance must MATCH the LOAD impedance. If they're mismatched:
- Power is reflected back rather than transferred
- Energy is wasted
- The system operates inefficiently

This is why audio systems have impedance ratings (8 ohms, etc.) - the amplifier and speakers must match. It's why radio antennas must be tuned - the transmitter and antenna impedance must match for maximum signal radiation.

**Input vs Output Impedance**

Every circuit has:
- **Input impedance** - how it appears to signals coming IN
- **Output impedance** - how it appears to signals going OUT
