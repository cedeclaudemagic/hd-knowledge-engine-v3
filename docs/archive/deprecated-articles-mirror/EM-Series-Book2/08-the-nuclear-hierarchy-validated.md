## The Nuclear Hierarchy

### What Is Nuclear Transformation?

Every hexagram contains a "nuclear hexagram"—an inner pattern extracted from its middle lines. To find it:

1. Take lines 2, 3, 4 → lower nuclear trigram
2. Take lines 3, 4, 5 → upper nuclear trigram
3. Combine them → nuclear hexagram

```
Original hexagram:    Line 6
                      Line 5  ─┐
                      Line 4  ─┼─ Upper nuclear trigram
                      Line 3  ─┼─┼─ Lower nuclear trigram
                      Line 2  ─┘ │
                      Line 1     │
                                 ▼
                      Nuclear hexagram
```

This transformation can be applied repeatedly. Eventually, every hexagram "descends" to one of four gates that transform only into themselves or each other: Gates 1, 2, 63, 64.

These are the Pillars—the fixed points and cycles of nuclear transformation.

### The Three Levels

| Level | Count | Description |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| Pillars | 4 | Gates 1, 2, 63, 64 — nuclear fixed points and cycles |
| Mysteries | 12 | First-generation descendants of Pillars |
| Letters | 48 | All remaining gates |

Each Pillar generates 3 Mysteries (12 total). Each Mystery governs 4 Letters (48 total). The hierarchy accounts for all 64 gates: 4 + 12 + 48 = 64.

## Planetary Polarity by Pillar

Human Design assigns planetary "exaltations" and "detriments" to each of the 384 lines (64 gates × 6 lines). These assignments indicate which planets are strengthened or challenged at each position.

We tested whether planetary assignments follow Pillar structure.

### The Polarity Test

For each planet, we counted:
- Exaltations per Pillar
- Detriments per Pillar
- Net affinity = Exaltations - Detriments

If planets distribute randomly, net affinity should be near zero for all Pillars.

### Results

| Planet | Fire | Water | Truth | Light | Pattern |
|--------|------|-------|-------|-------|---------|
| Sun | +10 | +16 | +12 | +5 | Always positive |
| Mars | -13 | -11 | -21 | -20 | Always negative |
| Mercury | -7 | -10 | -3 | -9 | Always negative |
| Saturn | +7 | +7 | +4 | +4 | Always positive |
| Pluto | +9 | -8 | +7 | +2 | **Discriminates** |
| Venus | -7 | +4 | +3 | +8 | **Discriminates** |

**Statistical significance: Z = 7.23 (p < 0.0000000001)**

The probability of this polarity pattern occurring by chance is effectively zero.

### Interpretation

Planets fall into two types:

**Universal planets** (same polarity in all Pillars):
- Sun: Always exalted (positive in all Pillars)
- Mars: Always challenged (negative in all Pillars)
- Saturn: Always exalted
- Mercury: Always challenged

**Discriminating planets** (polarity varies by Pillar):
- Pluto: Strong positive in Fire/Truth, negative in Water
- Venus: Positive in Water/Light, negative in Fire

The universal planets ignore Pillar structure—they have consistent character everywhere.
The discriminating planets *respond* to Pillar structure—their expression depends on context.

## Family Differentiation

Each Mystery contains one standing wave and several cross-zero gates. We tested whether cross-zero gates inherit planetary patterns from their standing wave sibling.

### The Hypothesis

If nuclear families share planetary patterns, cross-zero gates should have similar line-by-line planets to their standing wave.

### The Result

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Observed inheritance | 3.9% |
| Random baseline | 12.2% |
| Ratio | 0.32× (below random) |
| Z-score | -1.99 |

**Cross-zero siblings show anti-inheritance.** They are significantly *less* likely to share planets with their standing wave than random unrelated gates.

### Interpretation

Nuclear families don't share planetary patterns—they *differentiate*. Each family member occupies a different planetary niche, maximising coverage rather than creating redundancy.

This is structurally informative: the nuclear hierarchy encodes *differentiation*, not *inheritance*. Knowing the standing wave's planets tells you what the siblings will *avoid*, not what they'll share.

The nuclear hierarchy tells us *something* about planetary distribution. It doesn't tell us *everything*.

The anti-inheritance finding (Z = -1.99) reveals how nuclear families work: not by sharing patterns but by dividing territory. This is information-theoretic efficiency—families maximise coverage.
