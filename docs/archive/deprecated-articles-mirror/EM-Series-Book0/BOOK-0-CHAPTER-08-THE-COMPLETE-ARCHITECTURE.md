## The Five Shapes

There are exactly five perfectly symmetrical three-dimensional shapes. The ancient Greeks called them the Platonic solids. They are:

| Shape | Vertices | Faces | Built From |
|-------|----------|-------|------------|
| Tetrahedron | 4 | 4 triangles | Simplest 3D form |
| Cube | 8 | 6 squares | Binary coordinates |
| Octahedron | 6 | 8 triangles | Dual of cube |
| Dodecahedron | 20 | 12 pentagons | Phi proportions |
| Icosahedron | 12 | 20 triangles | Dual of dodecahedron |

These five shapes nest inside each other. And each one maps to a component of the I Ching.

---

## The Mapping

**Tetrahedron (4 vertices) → 4 Bigrams**

Before trigrams, there are bigrams — two-line patterns. Four possibilities: 00, 01, 10, 11.

These are the most fundamental divisions:
- 00: Pure yin (old yin)
- 11: Pure yang (old yang)
- 01: Young yang (becoming yang)
- 10: Young yin (becoming yin)

In some traditions, these are called the Four Pillars. Four vertices. Four primordial states.

The tetrahedron is the simplest 3D shape — and the bigrams are the simplest I Ching elements.

---

**Cube (8 vertices) → 8 Trigrams**

We've explored this thoroughly. Eight corners, each addressed by three binary coordinates. Eight trigrams, each a three-line pattern.

The cube is the skeleton. The trigrams are the state space.

---

**Octahedron (6 vertices) → 6 Lines**

Here's something new.

The octahedron is the *dual* of the cube. If you place a point at the centre of each face of a cube and connect them, you get an octahedron.

The cube has 6 faces. The octahedron has 6 vertices. They're complementary shapes.

```
    Cube (6 faces)          Octahedron (6 vertices)
    
        ●───────●               ●
       /|      /|              /|\
      / |     / |             / | \
     ●───────●  |            ●──●──●
     |  ●───|───●             \ | /
     | /    | /                \|/
     ●───────●                  ●
```

Now, a hexagram has 6 lines. Each line is a position from bottom (1) to top (6).

The 6 lines map to the 6 vertices of the octahedron — the 6 directions perpendicular to the cube faces:

| Line | Direction | Meaning |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Line 1 | -Z (down) | Foundation, root |
| Line 2 | -Y (back) | Inner development |
| Line 3 | -X (left) | Threshold, crisis |
| Line 4 | +X (right) | Entering new territory |
| Line 5 | +Y (front) | Full expression |
| Line 6 | +Z (up) | Completion, transcendence |

The octahedron gives us the directions. The cube gives us the positions. Together, they describe the full hexagram: *where* you are (trigrams) and *what stage* you're at (lines).

---

**Icosahedron (12 vertices) → 12 Profiles**

In Human Design, there are 12 Profiles — combinations of conscious and unconscious line positions (1/3, 1/4, 2/4, 2/5, 3/5, 3/6, 4/6, 4/1, 5/1, 5/2, 6/2, 6/3).

The icosahedron has 12 vertices.

The icosahedron is built on phi — its proportions are golden. And profiles describe how you *engage* with life, how consciousness meets its vehicle.

This is Level 2: the phi layer, governing how the binary structure interfaces with lived experience.

---

**Dodecahedron (20 vertices) → The Container**

The dodecahedron contains everything else. 20 vertices, of which 8 form the inscribed cube.

It's the space in which the I Ching lives. The phi-governed container for the binary-governed structure.

The dodecahedron doesn't map to a specific I Ching element. It *is* the totality — the embedding space, the context, the field.

## Duality

Notice the pattern of duals:

- Cube ↔ Octahedron (swap vertices and faces)
- Dodecahedron ↔ Icosahedron (swap vertices and faces)
- Tetrahedron ↔ Tetrahedron (self-dual)

Duality means: what's a corner on one shape is a face on the other.

In I Ching terms:
- Trigrams (cube vertices) relate to Lines (octahedron vertices)
- A trigram is a *state*; a line is a *direction*
- They're complementary perspectives on the same structure

The cube gives you "where." The octahedron gives you "which way."

## What This Means

The I Ching isn't a collection of 64 arbitrary symbols with memorised meanings.

It's a **geometric engine**:

1. **The cube** defines the 8 fundamental states (trigrams)
2. **The octahedron** defines the 6 stages of development (lines)
3. **The tetrahedra** divide the cube into Void and Material
4. **The path** through the cube generates the wave
5. **The axis** creates the spiral
6. **The offset** creates the double helix
7. **The dodecahedron** contains it all in golden proportion
8. **The icosahedron** governs how we interface with it

Every piece interlocks. Every number has geometric meaning. Every relationship is structural, not arbitrary.

## The Meanings Emerge

This is the key insight:

**Hexagram meanings aren't assigned. They emerge.**

When you understand:
- Which trigrams are involved (cube positions)
- What type of movement that is (standing wave, cross-zero, same-phase)
- What line you're reading (octahedron direction)
- What stage of the journey (1-6)
- What circuit it belongs to (structural grouping)

...the meaning becomes *readable*. Not from memorisation, but from geometry.

Hexagram 11 means "Peace" because Heaven below and Earth above creates convergent flow. That's not poetry — that's cube mechanics.

Hexagram 12 means "Standstill" because Earth below and Heaven above creates divergent flow. Geometry again.

The ancients encoded this. We're just learning to read it.
