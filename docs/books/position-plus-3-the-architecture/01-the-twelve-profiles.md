# The Twelve Profiles

*First chapter of Position +3: The Architecture*

---

## The Way You Learn

Watch someone approach a new subject.

One person immediately digs in. They want the foundation, the basics, the underlying principles. They'll read the manual before touching the machine. They need to *know* before they'll move.

Another person waits to be called. They have a natural gift — everyone can see it — but they won't offer it unsolicited. They need to be drawn out, recognised, invited to share what they somehow already know.

A third person just tries things. They'll break it to understand it. They learn through trial and error, through bonds made and broken, through the accumulated wisdom of what doesn't work.

These aren't personality types. They're not learning styles in the educational psychology sense. They're something more fundamental: *orientations in hexagram space*.

Human Design calls them Lines. There are six of them. And when you combine two Lines — one conscious, one unconscious — you get a Profile. There are exactly twelve Profiles.

Not eleven. Not thirteen. Twelve.

Why? The answer is geometric, and it reveals something profound about the architecture of human experience.

## The Octahedron

Picture a three-dimensional shape with six vertices — six points. Connect them so that every vertex links to four others, with two vertices remaining opposite each other, as far apart as possible.

What you've described is an octahedron. It looks like two pyramids joined at their bases. It has eight faces (all triangles), twelve edges, and six vertices.

Those six vertices are the six Lines.

```
           Line 6
              ●
             /|\
            / | \
           /  |  \
    Line 3 ●──┼──● Line 4
           \  |  /
            \ | /
             \|/
              ●
           Line 1
              
      Line 2 ●     ● Line 5
        (front)   (back)
```

This isn't metaphor. The six Lines of Human Design map precisely onto the six vertices of an octahedron. The relationships between Lines — which ones are "harmonic," which ones create certain Profile dynamics — emerge directly from the geometry of this shape.

---

## Two Distances

Here's what matters for Profile: in an octahedron, there are only two distances between vertices.

**Edge distance:** Two vertices connected by an edge are relatively close. In a unit octahedron, this distance is √2 (about 1.414).

**Diameter distance:** Two vertices on opposite ends of the shape — passing through the centre — are maximally far apart. This distance is 2.

That's it. Two distances. No other options exist.

If you're standing at any vertex of an octahedron and you want to move to another vertex, you either take an edge (shorter distance) or you go through the centre to the opposite vertex (longer distance). There's no third choice.

Now here's the key: **Profile is created by connecting two Lines with a gap between them**. The gap can only be one of two sizes, because the octahedron only has two distances.

The smaller gap is **+2**. The larger gap is **+3**.

## The Twelve Profiles

With six Lines and two possible gaps, you might expect 6 × 2 = 12 Profiles. And that's exactly what we get.

**Gap +2 Profiles** (adjacent vertices — edge distance):
- 1/3 — Investigator / Martyr
- 2/4 — Hermit / Opportunist  
- 3/5 — Martyr / Heretic
- 4/6 — Opportunist / Role Model
- 5/1 — Heretic / Investigator
- 6/2 — Role Model / Hermit

**Gap +3 Profiles** (opposite vertices — diameter distance):
- 1/4 — Investigator / Opportunist
- 2/5 — Hermit / Heretic
- 3/6 — Martyr / Role Model
- 4/1 — Opportunist / Investigator
- 5/2 — Heretic / Hermit
- 6/3 — Role Model / Martyr

Count them. Twelve. Exactly twelve.

This isn't a design choice. It's geometric necessity. An octahedron has six vertices and two distances. Therefore, twelve — and only twelve — Profiles can exist.

## Why the Gap Matters

The gap isn't just a number. It describes the *relationship* between your conscious and unconscious orientations.

**Gap +2** creates Profiles where the two Lines are *adjacent* on the octahedron. They're neighbours. They share edges with common vertices. There's a kind of natural flow between them — they're working nearby territory.

The 1/3, for instance, combines the Investigator (Line 1) with the Martyr (Line 3). Both are in the lower trigram — the foundational, personal, introspective realm. The 1 investigates; the 3 experiments. Different approaches, but they're neighbours on the shape. The Profile has coherence.

**Gap +3** creates Profiles where the two Lines are *opposite* on the octahedron. They're across from each other, passing through the centre. They're as far apart as two vertices can be.

The 1/4, for instance, combines the Investigator (Line 1) with the Opportunist (Line 4). Line 1 is foundation, depth, going inward. Line 4 is network, influence, going outward. They're geometric opposites. The Profile contains a polarity — a built-in tension between two maximally different orientations.

This is why the +3 Profiles often feel more complex, more dynamic, sometimes more challenging. They're holding opposites together. The +2 Profiles feel more integrated, more flowing — their Lines are working adjacent territory.

Neither is better. They're different geometries.

## The Harmonic Pairs

The octahedron reveals something else: which Lines are natural partners.

Look at the shape. Line 1 and Line 4 are opposite vertices — directly across from each other through the centre. So are Line 2 and Line 5. So are Line 3 and Line 6.

These are the **harmonic pairs**:
- 1 and 4
- 2 and 5
- 3 and 6

Harmonic pairs have a special relationship. They're not similar — they're *complementary*. Each completes what the other lacks. The 1's depth needs the 4's network to spread. The 2's natural gift needs the 5's projection to be seen. The 3's experimentation needs the 6's perspective to find meaning.

This isn't poetic interpretation. It's geometry. Opposite vertices on an octahedron define an axis — a complete dimension of the shape. Each harmonic pair represents one of the three axes of the octahedron.

When you have a +3 Profile (1/4, 2/5, 3/6, 4/1, 5/2, 6/3), your conscious and unconscious Lines are a harmonic pair. You contain both ends of an axis within yourself. That's a particular kind of wholeness — and a particular kind of tension.

## The Three Categories

There's one more geometric feature that matters: the division between lower and upper trigrams.

In hexagram structure, Lines 1, 2, and 3 form the lower trigram. Lines 4, 5, and 6 form the upper trigram. This division is fundamental to the I Ching and carries into Human Design.

The lower trigram is *personal*. It's about your inner process, your foundation, your individual journey.

The upper trigram is *transpersonal*. It's about your role in the larger pattern, your impact on others, your place in the collective.

This creates three categories of Profile:

**Right Angle** (Lines 1, 2, 3 — lower trigram): Personal destiny. Your Profile is about your own journey. You're here for your own experience, your own development. Karma is personal.

**Left Angle** (Lines 5, 6 — upper trigram): Transpersonal destiny. Your Profile is about your role in others' journeys. You're here to participate in something larger. Karma is collective.

**Juxtaposition** (Line 4 — the bridge): Fixed fate. Line 4 sits at the transition point between trigrams. It's neither purely personal nor purely transpersonal. It's the hinge.

The twelve Profiles distribute across these categories:
- Right Angle: 1/3, 1/4, 2/4, 2/5, 3/5, 3/6
- Juxtaposition: 4/1
- Left Angle: 4/6, 5/1, 5/2, 6/2, 6/3

Notice that 4/1 is the only Juxtaposition Profile. Line 4 appears in the first position only once — creating a unique Profile that's neither Right Angle nor Left Angle but something in between.

## What This Means

Your Profile isn't a personality description. It's a geometric fact.

When someone says "I'm a 3/5," they're saying: "My conscious orientation is Line 3 (Martyr — learning through trial and error), and my unconscious orientation is Line 5 (Heretic — carrying projection and providing practical solutions). The gap between them is +2, meaning they're adjacent on the octahedron. I'm a Right Angle Profile, so my path is primarily personal."

This isn't belief. It's not interpretation. It's geometric description — as precise as saying "I'm standing at the corner of Fifth and Main."

The *meaning* of being a 3/5 — what it feels like, how it plays out, what challenges and gifts it brings — that's where interpretation enters. But the *structure* is mathematical. The octahedron doesn't care what you believe. It has six vertices and two distances. Therefore twelve Profiles. Therefore your Profile.

## The Liberation

Here's what changes when you understand Profile geometrically:

You stop asking "Why am I like this?" as if it were a cosmic injustice or a karmic punishment. You're a 6/2 because consciousness, moving through hexagram space, can only orient in six directions, and the distance between your conscious and unconscious orientations can only be one of two values. You're a 6/2 because geometry.

You stop trying to be a different Profile. A 1/3 can't become a 5/1 any more than the north pole can relocate to the equator. The structure is what it is. The question isn't whether you like your Profile — it's whether you understand it well enough to live it correctly.

You start recognising others' Profiles as geometric facts rather than personality judgments. The 4/6 who networks constantly isn't being superficial — they're living their geometry. The 5/1 who gets projected upon isn't seeking attention — they're occupying a vertex that attracts projection.

The octahedron doesn't judge. It simply structures. And you're one of twelve possible orientations within that structure.

Not because of karma. Not because of past lives. Not because of anything you did or failed to do.

Because six vertices. Because two distances. Because geometry.
