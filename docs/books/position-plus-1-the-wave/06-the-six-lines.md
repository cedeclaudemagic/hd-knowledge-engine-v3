# The Six Lines

*Sixth in a series exploring the electromagnetic structure of consciousness*

---

## Where We Are

We've learned that hexagrams are movements — journeys from one position on the wave to another. We've classified them into three types: standing waves that hold position, cross-zero movements that transform between domains, and same-side movements that circulate within a domain.

But so far we've treated each hexagram as a single arrow. Start here, end there. One movement.

Now we look closer. Because the journey isn't instantaneous. It unfolds. It has stages. It develops through six distinct moments.

These are the six lines.

---

## The Structure of the Hexagram

A hexagram is six lines stacked vertically. Each line is either yang (solid) or yin (broken). But they're not six independent elements — they're organised into two groups.

**Lines 1, 2, and 3** — the bottom half — form the **inner trigram**.

**Lines 4, 5, and 6** — the top half — form the **outer trigram**.

The inner trigram is where the journey begins. The outer trigram is where it's heading.

```
Line 6  ━━━━━━━━━  ┐
Line 5  ━━━  ━━━   │ Outer Trigram (destination)
Line 4  ━━━━━━━━━  ┘
        ─────────── threshold
Line 3  ━━━  ━━━   ┐
Line 2  ━━━━━━━━━  │ Inner Trigram (origin)
Line 1  ━━━  ━━━   ┘
```

The hexagram isn't just two trigrams stacked. It's a journey *from* one *to* the other. And the six lines trace that journey step by step.

## The Journey Through Six Stages

Each line represents a stage in the movement from origin to destination.

**Line 1: Entry**

The beginning. The first engagement with the inner trigram's position. You're arriving at the starting point, orienting yourself, establishing initial contact with where you are.

Line 1 is tentative. Foundational. The seed that contains everything but has expressed nothing yet. In traditional I Ching interpretation, Line 1 often describes hidden potential, initial conditions, the situation before action.

**Line 2: Development**

Now you're working within the inner trigram. No longer just arriving — inhabiting. The quality of the starting position is expressing itself, developing, becoming real in your experience.

Line 2 is the inner trigram flowering. The middle line of the lower three, it's the heart of where you begin. Traditional interpretations often show capability developing, relationships forming, the middle path within your current state.

**Line 3: Completion of Origin**

The inner trigram reaches its fullest expression. You've gone as far as you can go in the starting position. Everything that can develop here has developed. The origin is complete.

But Line 3 is also restless. It sits at the top of the lower trigram, pressing against the threshold. Traditional interpretations often show tension, danger, the difficulty of a position that's complete but not yet transformed. There's nowhere left to go *within* — the only movement now is *across*.

**— The Threshold —**

Between Line 3 and Line 4 lies the most important transition in the hexagram.

This is where you leave the inner trigram and enter the outer. This is where origin becomes destination. This is where — in cross-zero hexagrams — you pass through the still point and transform.

The 3→4 threshold isn't a line. It's a crossing. A discontinuity. The space between what you were and what you're becoming.

**Line 4: Entry into Destination**

You've crossed. Now you're in the outer trigram — but just barely. Line 4 is the first step into new territory. The destination position is beginning to express, but it's not established yet.

Line 4 is often described as precarious. You're no longer in the familiar inner trigram, but you haven't consolidated the outer. Traditional interpretations show adaptation, finding footing, the vulnerability of fresh arrival.

**Line 5: Development of Destination**

Now you're inhabiting the outer trigram. The destination is flowering, expressing its nature, becoming real. This is often considered the most favourable line — you've made the crossing and are now developing fully in your new position.

Line 5 is the heart of the outer trigram. The ruler of the hexagram, in traditional terms. The position of maximum expression of where you've arrived.

**Line 6: Completion**

The outer trigram reaches its fullest expression. The journey is complete. You've arrived fully at the destination.

But Line 6 carries a shadow. It's the end. There's nowhere further to go in this direction. Traditional interpretations often show culmination tipping into excess, completion becoming rigidity, the danger of going too far.

Line 6 completes the hexagram — but completion isn't always comfortable.

## The Threshold in Each Type

The 3→4 transition means different things depending on which type of movement the hexagram is.

**In standing waves**, there is no transition.

The inner and outer trigrams are identical. Line 3 and Line 4 are in the same position. There's no crossing because there's nowhere to cross *to*.

Standing waves don't journey. Lines 1-3 and Lines 4-6 both express the same position — just from different angles. The "inner" and "outer" aren't origin and destination; they're inner and outer expressions of the same state.

This is why standing wave interpretations speak in terms of being rather than becoming. There's no threshold because there's no movement.

**In same-side movements**, the threshold shifts expression but not domain.

You move from one position to another, but both positions are on the same side of zero. The crossing at 3→4 is real — you leave the origin and enter the destination — but it's not a transformation. You stay in form, or you stay in potential.

The 3→4 transition in same-side hexagrams often feels like a *change of mode* within continuity. You're still in the same domain, but you're expressing differently. Like moving from storage to flow, or from gate to pole. Development, not metamorphosis.

**In cross-zero movements**, the threshold is transformation.

The inner trigram is on one side of zero. The outer trigram is on the other. The 3→4 transition crosses the still point. This is where energy changes domain — where potential becomes form, or form becomes potential.

The 3→4 threshold in cross-zero hexagrams is the moment of metamorphosis. Before it, you're in one domain. After it, you're in another. The crossing cannot be gradual. It's a phase transition, a quantum leap, a discontinuous shift in the nature of what's moving.

This is why cross-zero hexagrams often have such dramatic Line 4s. You've just transformed. Everything is different. You're finding your footing in a new mode of existence.

## Reading the Lines

Now we can read any line with understanding.

To interpret a line, ask:

1. **Which stage?**
   - Lines 1-3: Still in origin (inner trigram)
   - Line 3 specifically: Pressing against threshold
   - Lines 4-6: Now in destination (outer trigram)
   - Line 4 specifically: Just crossed threshold

2. **What type of hexagram?**
   - Standing wave: No threshold, pure expression of position
   - Same-side: Threshold shifts mode but not domain
   - Cross-zero: Threshold transforms domain

3. **What positions?**
   - Inner trigram tells you *where the journey starts*
   - Outer trigram tells you *where it's heading*
   - The positions give meaning to each stage

## An Example: Hexagram 1, The Creative

Heaven over Heaven. Position -4 to position -4. A standing wave at the source.

**Line 1:** "Hidden dragon. Do not act."

The entry into pure potential. At position -4, the source of all creativity, but just beginning to engage with it. The dragon is there but hidden — potential not yet expressing. Don't act because action requires movement toward form, and this line is about *being* at the source, not leaving it.

**Line 2:** "Dragon appearing in the field. It furthers one to see the great person."

Now developing within pure potential. The dragon becomes visible — potential expressing more fully, but still entirely in the domain of the unmanifest. The "great person" to see is one who embodies creative potential. Still no action, but presence becoming clear.

**Line 3:** "The superior person is creatively active all day. At nightfall, still alert. Danger. No blame."

The completion of the inner trigram. Maximum expression of source potential while still at the source. "Creatively active all day" — potential fully expressing. But "at nightfall, still alert" — even at completion, there's no rest, no crossing. The danger is that of a position fully expressed but going nowhere. No blame, because this is a standing wave: there's nowhere to go.

**Line 4:** "Wavering flight over the depths. No blame."

Entry into the outer trigram — but in a standing wave, the outer is the same position as the inner. So what changes? The *expression* changes. Lines 1-3 were inner experience of source. Lines 4-6 are outer expression of source. "Wavering flight" — the creative now relating to the world, not just being itself. "Over the depths" — source potential engaging with possibility of manifestation. Still no blame, still no crossing. Just the outer face of the same position.

**Line 5:** "Flying dragon in the heavens. It furthers one to see the great person."

Full development of the outer expression. The dragon now flies in heaven — creative potential fully expressed outwardly. This is the maximum external manifestation of position -4 while remaining at position -4. Pure creativity visible in the world.

**Line 6:** "Arrogant dragon will have cause to repent."

Completion becomes excess. The outer expression of pure potential taken too far. When creativity becomes arrogance — when the source position forgets it's a position and thinks it's the only position — there's regret. Line 6 warns: even the source has its limits. Even Heaven over Heaven can overreach.

## An Example: Hexagram 11, Peace

Earth over Heaven. Position +4 (form pole) to position -4 (potential pole). A cross-zero movement, dematerialising.

**Line 1:** "When ribbon grass is pulled up, the sod comes with it. Undertakings bring good fortune."

Entry into the form pole (+4). "Ribbon grass pulled up" — even the first engagement with maximum form begins to loosen things. This hexagram is moving *away* from form toward potential. So even at Line 1, the direction is set: form is going to release. "Undertakings bring good fortune" — in a dematerialising hexagram, action (undertaking) means releasing, which aligns with the direction.

**Line 2:** "Bearing with the uncultured, crossing the river without a boat, not neglecting what is distant, having no partisan concerns."

Developing within the form pole while oriented toward potential. "Bearing with the uncultured" — staying in form (+4) but with openness. "Crossing the river without a boat" — already the crossing is prefigured, even in Line 2. This hexagram is about release, so even while still in form, the quality is spaciousness, not rigidity.

**Line 3:** "No plain not followed by a slope. No going not followed by a return. Perseverance in difficulty brings no blame."

Completion of the inner trigram — last moment in maximum form before crossing toward potential. "No plain not followed by a slope" — form is about to give way. "No going not followed by a return" — the journey into potential is a return, not a departure. Difficulty because this is the threshold: the last grip of form before release.

**— Threshold: crossing zero, form to potential —**

**Line 4:** "Fluttering down, not boasting of wealth, sharing spontaneously with neighbours."

Just crossed into the potential domain. "Fluttering down" — the movement from form toward source feels like descent, like release of weight. "Not boasting of wealth" — form's accumulations are being released. "Sharing spontaneously" — what was held in form now flows freely. This is the immediate experience of having crossed zero: lightness, release, spontaneous generosity.

**Line 5:** "The sovereign gives his daughter in marriage. This brings blessing and supreme good fortune."

Full development in the potential domain. "Gives his daughter" — further release, further letting go. The sovereign (maximum power in form) releases even what's most precious. "Blessing and supreme good fortune" — because this hexagram's direction IS release. Line 5 in Peace is maximum alignment with the movement. Pure letting go.

**Line 6:** "The wall falls back into the moat. Use no army now."

Completion of the journey to potential. "The wall falls" — the last structures of form collapse. "Into the moat" — returning to formlessness. "Use no army" — no defending, no holding, no resistance. The dematerialisation is complete. What was form is now fully released.

## The Line Position Tells the Story

Notice how knowing the structure changes interpretation.

Without the framework, you read each line as a separate oracle. With the framework, you see each line as a stage in a journey:

- **Lines 1-3:** Where you begin, developing toward threshold
- **Threshold:** The crossing (or non-crossing, in standing waves)
- **Lines 4-6:** Where you arrive, developing toward completion

The position determines the context. The hexagram type determines what the threshold means. The line number determines where you are in the journey.

This isn't memorisation. It's geometry. Once you see the structure, the meanings unfold from it.
