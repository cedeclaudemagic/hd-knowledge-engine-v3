# Chapter 3: The Wave Emerges

---

We have a cube. We have eight corners named for the eight trigrams. We have two tetrahedra — Void and Material — interpenetrating at the centre.

Now let's make it move.

## A Path Through the Corners

Imagine walking through the cube, visiting each corner once before returning to the start. Not randomly — in a specific order. A path.

There are many possible paths through eight corners. But one path is special. One path has been encoded in the I Ching for three thousand years.

Here it is:

```
Lake → Fire → Thunder → Earth → Mountain → Water → Wind → Heaven → (back to Lake)
```

Eight steps. Eight corners. A complete circuit.

This isn't arbitrary. This is the sequence that the ancient Chinese arrangement encodes — the order in which the trigrams appear when you read around the wheel that organises the 64 hexagrams.

But why this particular path? What makes it special?

Watch what happens when we walk it.

---

## Giving the Corners Height

To see the pattern, we need to give each corner a *height* — a position on a vertical scale.

Remember the two families:
- **Material family** (Earth's tetrahedron): Earth, Thunder, Water, Mountain
- **Void family** (Heaven's tetrahedron): Wind, Fire, Lake, Heaven

The Material family has more broken lines. In the Chinese tradition, broken lines are *yin* — receptive, dense, manifest. Let's place these corners in the **positive** region of our scale. They represent form, substance, the crystallised.

The Void family has more solid lines. Solid lines are *yang* — creative, energetic, potential. Let's place these corners in the **negative** region. They represent energy, potential, the unmanifest.

(This may seem backwards — why is "creative" negative? Because we're measuring *density*, not value. The Void is less dense. Form is more dense. The scale runs from empty to full.)

Now, within each family, some corners are more extreme than others:

**Material family (positive):**
- Earth (000): Maximum density. Three broken lines. Position: **+4**
- Mountain (001): High density. Position: **+3**
- Water (010): Medium density. Position: **+2**
- Thunder (100): Just past centre. Position: **+1**

**Void family (negative):**
- Wind (011): Just past centre. Position: **-1**
- Fire (101): Medium void. Position: **-2**
- Lake (110): Deep void. Position: **-3**
- Heaven (111): Maximum void. Three solid lines. Position: **-4**

```
Position Scale:

    +4  ●  Earth (000)      ← Maximum Material
    +3  ●  Mountain (001)
    +2  ●  Water (010)
    +1  ●  Thunder (100)
     0  ────────────────────  ← Centre (the threshold)
    -1  ●  Wind (011)
    -2  ●  Fire (101)
    -3  ●  Lake (110)
    -4  ●  Heaven (111)      ← Maximum Void
```

Eight positions. Four above zero, four below. Perfectly balanced.

Now let's walk the path and track our height at each step:

| 4 | Earth | +4 |
| 5 | Mountain | +3 |
| 6 | Water | +2 |

Do you see it?

**A wave.**

Starting in the void (Lake, -3), rising through Fire (-2), crossing zero at Thunder (+1), peaking at Earth (+4), descending through Mountain (+3) and Water (+2), crossing zero again at Wind (-1), reaching the trough at Heaven (-4).

Then back to Lake, and the cycle repeats.

This isn't something we imposed on the cube. This is what *emerges* when you walk through its corners in the ancient sequence.

---

## The Zero Crossings

Look at where the wave crosses zero — the threshold between Void and Material:

**First crossing:** Fire (-2) → Thunder (+1)
- Moving from Void into Material
- From potential into form
- The moment of *manifestation*

**Second crossing:** Water (+2) → Wind (-1)
- Moving from Material into Void
- From form back to potential
- The moment of *dissolution*

Two crossings per cycle. One in each direction.

This is the breath of existence:
- Inhale: Void → Material (things come into form)
- Exhale: Material → Void (things return to potential)

The wave breathes. Twice per cycle. In, out. Manifest, dissolve.

---

## What Kind of Wave?

In mathematics, a wave that crosses zero twice per cycle and returns to its starting point is called a **fundamental oscillation**. It's the simplest possible wave — one complete up-and-down motion.

But there's something special about *this* wave.

Notice the path we walked:

```
Lake → Fire → Thunder → Earth → Mountain → Water → Wind → Heaven
```

We visited every corner exactly once. We never backtracked. We never skipped. A complete tour of all eight vertices before returning to the start.

In graph theory, this is called a **Hamiltonian cycle** — a path that visits every vertex of a shape exactly once and returns to the origin.

The I Ching's trigram sequence isn't just *a* path through the cube. It's the most elegant possible path — touching everything, wasting nothing, and generating a perfect wave in the process.

## The Wave as Carrier

Now we can understand something about the hexagrams.

Remember: a hexagram is two trigrams — lower and upper. In the I Ching wheel, the hexagrams are arranged in a specific order. As you move around the wheel, the *lower* trigram changes slowly while the *upper* trigram changes quickly.

The lower trigram follows our wave path: Lake → Fire → Thunder → Earth → Mountain → Water → Wind → Heaven. It dwells at each position for about eight hexagrams before moving to the next corner.

The upper trigram cycles through all eight positions much faster — visiting each corner while the lower trigram holds steady.

This is exactly how a **modulated wave** works:

- The **lower trigram** is the *carrier wave* — slow, fundamental, setting the basic oscillation
- The **upper trigram** is the *modulation* — fast, varied, encoding the detail

The I Ching wheel is an AM radio signal frozen in symbol.

## Why This Matters

The wave isn't decoration. It's function.

When you cast a hexagram, you're not just getting a random symbol. You're getting a *position* on this wave — a specific point in the cycle of manifestation and dissolution.

Is your hexagram in the Material domain (above zero)? You're dealing with form, structure, the crystallised.

Is it in the Void domain (below zero)? You're dealing with potential, energy, the unmanifest.

Is it near a zero crossing? You're at a threshold — things are transforming, crossing from one domain to the other.

Is it near a peak (+4) or trough (-4)? You're at maximum — the extreme of form or the extreme of formlessness.

The wave tells you *where you are* in the great oscillation of change.

## The Cube Becomes Dynamic

We started with a static shape — eight corners, twelve edges, six faces. Now we see that shape isn't static at all.

The cube encodes a *process*. A movement. A rhythm.

Walk its corners in the right sequence, and you trace a wave. The wave breathes — in and out, manifest and dissolve, form and emptiness.

The I Ching isn't a collection of 64 separate symbols. It's a single wave, frozen at 64 different moments. Each hexagram is a snapshot. Together, they show the whole motion.

But we're not done.

The wave we've traced is flat — it lives on a two-dimensional graph. Position against step. Up and down.

What if we wrapped that wave around? What if we let the cube *rotate* on its axis?

Something new would appear. Something three-dimensional. Something alive.

A helix.
