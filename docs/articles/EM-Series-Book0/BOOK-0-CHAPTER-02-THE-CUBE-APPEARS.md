# Chapter 2: The Cube Appears

---

Let's build it.

Take a cube — any cube. A box, a die, a child's building block. Hold it in your hands. Look at it.

Eight corners. Twelve edges. Six faces.

Now let's give those corners names.

---

## Placing the First Two

Pick one corner. Call it Earth.

Earth is the trigram made of three broken lines. In binary: 000. In the Chinese tradition: the receptive, the yielding, pure matter, maximum density.

Now find the corner diagonally opposite — the one as far from Earth as possible. You'd have to travel through the centre of the cube to get there. Call this corner Heaven.

Heaven is three solid lines. In binary: 111. In the tradition: the creative, the initiating, pure energy, maximum potential.

```
                Heaven (111)
                    ●

                  / | \
                 /  |  \
                /   |   \
               /    |    \

             /      |      \
            ●-------+-------●

        |   ●-------+---|---●
        |  /        |   |  /
        | /         |   | /
        |/          |   |/
        ●-----------+---●
                    |
                    ●
                Earth (000)
```

Earth and Heaven. 000 and 111. The two poles of the cube.

Every bit is different between them. Every coordinate flipped. As far apart as geometry allows.

Already, something is encoded: **maximum opposition is maximum distance**.

---

## The Two Families

Now look at the six remaining corners.

Three of them are adjacent to Earth — connected by a single edge. These are the corners where only *one* coordinate changes from Earth's (0,0,0):

- **(1,0,0)** — Thunder. One solid line at the bottom.
- **(0,1,0)** — Water. One solid line in the middle.
- **(0,0,1)** — Mountain. One solid line at the top.

Three trigrams. Each with exactly one solid line. Each one step away from Earth.

The other three corners are adjacent to Heaven — connected by a single edge. These are the corners where only *one* coordinate changes from Heaven's (1,1,1):

- **(0,1,1)** — Wind. One broken line at the bottom.
- **(1,0,1)** — Fire. One broken line in the middle.
- **(1,1,0)** — Lake. One broken line at the top.

Three trigrams. Each with exactly one broken line. Each one step away from Heaven.

```
        Heaven's Family               Earth's Family
        (2 solid lines each)          (1 solid line each)
        
             Heaven (111)                  Earth (000)
                 ●                             ●
                /|\                           /|\
               / | \                         / | \
              /  |  \                       /  |  \
           Wind Fire Lake              Thunder Water Mountain
           (011)(101)(110)              (100)  (010)  (001)
```

Two families. Two neighbourhoods. The corners near Heaven and the corners near Earth.

## The Hidden Structure

Now here's where it gets interesting.

Look at the cube again. Focus on the four corners of Heaven's family: Heaven itself, plus Wind, Fire, and Lake.

These four corners form a shape. Connect them with lines. What do you see?

A triangle in three dimensions. A **tetrahedron** — a pyramid with a triangular base.

```
              Heaven (111)
                  ●
                 /|\
                / | \
               /  |  \
              /   |   \
           Wind---+---Lake
           (011)  |  (110)
              \   |   /
               \  |  /
                \ | /
                 \|/
                  ●
              Fire (101)
```

Four corners. Four triangular faces. The simplest possible three-dimensional shape.

Now look at Earth's family: Earth, Thunder, Water, Mountain.

Another tetrahedron. Another four-cornered pyramid.

The cube contains two tetrahedra. They share the same centre but point in opposite directions. They interpenetrate, like two pyramids passing through each other.

## The Star

If you could see both tetrahedra at once — one pointing "up" toward Heaven, one pointing "down" toward Earth — you would see a six-pointed star in three dimensions.

This shape has a name. In sacred geometry, it's called the **Star Tetrahedron** or **Merkaba**. It appears in mystical traditions across cultures.

But we didn't invoke mysticism to find it. We just placed eight binary patterns on a cube and looked at what was already there.

The Star Tetrahedron isn't a symbol *assigned* to the I Ching. It's the literal geometry that *emerges* when you map trigrams to cube corners.

```
                    ●  Heaven

             / Wind|Fire\ \

      Thunder      ●      Mountain
       (100)     Lake      (001)
                (110)

                  ●  Earth
               Water
               (010)
```

Two tetrahedra. One made of corners with mostly solid lines (Heaven's family). One made of corners with mostly broken lines (Earth's family).

Let's give these families clearer names:

- **Heaven's tetrahedron**: Wind, Fire, Lake, Heaven — *the Void family*
- **Earth's tetrahedron**: Thunder, Water, Mountain, Earth — *the Material family*

Void and Material. The two halves of existence. Encoded in the geometry of a cube.

## The Pairs

Now something remarkable.

Look at Thunder (100) and Wind (011). Add their binary codes digit by digit:

- 1 + 0 = 1
- 0 + 1 = 1  
- 0 + 1 = 1

Thunder + Wind = 111.

They're **complements**. Every bit that's 0 in one is 1 in the other. Together, they complete the full pattern.

And where are they on the cube? At opposite corners. Diagonally across from each other, passing through the centre.

Check the others:

- Earth (000) + Heaven (111) = 111 ✓ Opposite corners.
- Water (010) + Fire (101) = 111 ✓ Opposite corners.
- Mountain (001) + Lake (110) = 111 ✓ Opposite corners.

Every trigram has exactly one complement. Every complement pair sits at opposite corners of the cube. Four pairs, four diagonals, all passing through the centre.

```
The Four Diagonal Pairs:

    Earth (000) ←————————→ Heaven (111)
    Thunder (100) ←———————→ Wind (011)
    Water (010) ←————————→ Fire (101)
    Mountain (001) ←———————→ Lake (110)
```

This isn't coincidence. This is geometry.

**Complementarity is diagonal opposition.** The more different two things are, the further apart they sit in the cube. Maximum difference = maximum distance.

---

## Why This Matters

So far, we've established:

1. The 8 trigrams map perfectly to the 8 corners of a cube
2. The cube contains two tetrahedra — Void and Material families
3. Complementary trigrams sit at opposite corners

But why does this matter for *meaning*?

Consider what a hexagram is: two trigrams, lower and upper. A starting point and a destination. A movement through the cube.

If the lower trigram is Thunder and the upper trigram is Wind, you're moving from one corner to its opposite. Maximum distance. Maximum transformation. The full diagonal of the cube.

If the lower trigram is Thunder and the upper trigram is Water, you're moving between adjacent corners — one edge of the cube. A smaller shift. Less distance.

If the lower trigram is Thunder and the upper trigram is also Thunder, you're not moving at all. You're staying at the same corner. Pure state, held stable.

*The geometry of the movement determines the character of the hexagram.*

Big movements mean big change. Small movements mean development within a domain. No movement means stable being.

## A Preliminary Test

Let's check this against the actual I Ching.

**Hexagram 11: Peace (泰)**
- Lower trigram: Heaven (111) — the Void pole
- Upper trigram: Earth (000) — the Material pole

**Hexagram 12: Standstill (否)**
- Lower trigram: Earth (000) — the Material pole
- Upper trigram: Heaven (111) — the Void pole

Both hexagrams involve the same two corners — the maximum diagonal of the cube. But one is called "Peace" and the other "Standstill." What's different?

The *arrangement* — and what it means for natural flow.

In the Chinese understanding, Heaven's energy naturally *rises*. Earth's energy naturally *descends*. These are their intrinsic tendencies.

In **Hexagram 11**, Heaven sits below and Earth sits above. Heaven rises. Earth descends. They move *toward* each other. They meet in the middle. They mingle. Energy flows. *Peace*.

In **Hexagram 12**, Earth sits below and Heaven sits above. Earth sinks further down. Heaven rises further up. They move *apart*. No meeting. No exchange. Each retreats to its own pole. *Standstill*.

The geometry reveals it: it's not just which corners are involved, but whether their natural movements create **convergence** or **divergence**.

When the poles are arranged so their tendencies bring them together — peace.
When the poles are arranged so their tendencies drive them apart — stagnation.

The cube encodes not just position, but *dynamic relationship*.

## What We've Built

We started with a cube and eight symbols. Now we have:

- A three-dimensional map of the trigrams
- Two families (Void and Material) forming two tetrahedra
- Four complementary pairs at diagonal oppositions
- A way to understand hexagrams as movements with direction

And we've tested it: the meanings of Hexagram 11 and 12 emerge from the geometry.

But we've only scratched the surface.

The cube doesn't just sit there. It moves. It rotates. And when it does, something extraordinary happens.

A wave appears.
