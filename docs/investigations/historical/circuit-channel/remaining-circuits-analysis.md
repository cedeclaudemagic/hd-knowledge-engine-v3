### Why Tribal Circuits Have No Anchors

Both zero-anchor circuits are **tribal** (Defense and Ego). This makes functional sense:

| Property | Individual Circuits | Tribal Circuits |
|----------|--------------------|-----------------|
| Anchors | 1-3 | 0 |
| Reference frame | Internal/fixed | External/adaptive |
| Function | Self-coherence, recognition | Protection, agreements |
| Stability source | Standing wave anchors | Group dynamics |

**Tribal circuits derive stability from the collective, not from internal standing waves.**

Protection (Defense) requires responding to whatever threatens — a fixed reference would constrain response options. Willpower and agreements (Ego) require negotiating with others — fixed positions would prevent bargaining.

The zero-anchor property enables **maximum adaptive flexibility** at the cost of internal stability. Tribal circuits are stable because of their social context, not their electromagnetic architecture.

Integration and Centering are structural mirrors:

| Aspect | Integration | Centering |
|--------|-------------|-----------|
| Gates | 4 | 4 |
| Channels | 4 | 2 |
| Anchors | 1 | 1 |
| Anchor position | **-1 (Gate OUT)** | **+1 (Gate IN)** |
| Transformation % | 50% | 50% |
| Double transformers | 1 (commutator) | 0 |
| Function | Self-coherence | Individual direction |

**Mirror positions on the Gate axis:**

```
        VOID                              MATTER
          │                                  │
    Gate OUT                            Gate IN
      (-1)                                (+1)
        │                                  │
   Integration                         Centering
     anchor                              anchor
        │                                  │
        └──────────── GATES AXIS ──────────┘
```

Both circuits manage **individual switching**:
- Integration: switches self-states (who am I being now?)
- Centering: switches direction (where am I going now?)

The anchor positions reflect the switching direction:
- Gate OUT (-1): reference for releasing/completing states
- Gate IN (+1): reference for initiating/beginning directions

### The Tribal Exception

Zero-anchor circuits don't fit the simple inverse relationship (more anchors = less transformation). Instead, they occupy a distinct category:

- **Anchored circuits:** Stability from internal standing waves
- **Zero-anchor circuits:** Stability from external (social) context

Tribal circuits are electromagnetically "ungrounded" but socially grounded. Their flexibility is a feature, not a bug.
